# v0.7 calibration and sensitivity report

## Status and purpose

This is an engineering calibration report for the v0.7 synthetic closed-loop world. It verifies
provenance, deterministic regeneration, directional sensitivity, and stress-testable failure modes.
It is not a fit to retailer microdata, a demand forecast, or evidence of external predictive
validity.

The complete machine-readable result is
[`results/design/v0.7-calibration.json`](../results/design/v0.7-calibration.json). It is bound to its
contents by SHA-256 and can be checked with:

```bash
decision-agent-bench verify-closed-loop-calibration results/design/v0.7-calibration.json
```

Regenerate a report with `decision-agent-bench calibrate-closed-loop OUTPUT --days 28`. The
committed design uses three seeds, all five regimes, the public-information-matched policy, and
one-at-a-time parameter changes.

## Public grounding

| Construct | Public source | How it is used | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales, inventories, seasonality | [U.S. Census Monthly Retail Trade Survey](https://www.census.gov/retail/mrts/about_the_surveys.html) | Motivates store-level flows and explicit seasonal factors | Simulator levels are not fitted to confidential microdata |
| Own-price and cross-product response | [USDA ERS Food Demand Analysis](https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/food-choices-health/food-consumption-demand/food-demand-analysis) | Grounds response direction and informs elasticity ranges | Product coefficients remain synthetic |
| Perishable food loss | [USDA ERS Food Loss data](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-availability-per-capita-data-system/food-loss/) | Grounds higher loss exposure for perishables | Episode loss is intentionally stress-testable, not sector-estimated |
| Nominal food prices | [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/) | Motivates explicit price state and interventions | The benchmark does not forecast CPI |

All store, product, vendor, elasticity, demand, lead-time, return, disruption, and operational-event
values are synthetic unless the table above says range-informed. The sources constrain structure or
direction; they do not confer dataset representativeness.

## Declared sensitivity ranges

| Scale | Low | Base | High | Provenance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Demand | 0.80 | 1.00 | 1.20 | Synthetic |
| Price elasticity | 0.75 | 1.00 | 1.25 | USDA range-informed |
| Returns | 0.50 | 1.00 | 1.50 | Synthetic |
| Lead time | 0.75 | 1.00 | 1.50 | Synthetic |
| Disruption | 0.50 | 1.00 | 1.50 | Synthetic stress parameter |

For the committed seed under `stress_mixed`, increasing demand from 0.80 to 1.20 raises revenue
from 13,189.16 to 18,742.68 while reducing service level from 0.956408 to 0.925276. Increasing the
return-rate scale from 0.50 to 1.50 increases returned units from 30 to 81 and reduces gross profit
from 8,026.35 to 7,862.97. Increasing lead-time scale from 0.75 to 1.50 reduces service level from
0.947326 to 0.934039. These are directional mechanism checks, not effect-size estimates for the
retail sector.

Across three seeds, the five 28-day regimes produce different state digests and different stress
profiles. Mean service level ranges from 0.945529 to 0.946960, mean spoilage from 468.0 to 490.667,
and mean gross profit from 7,646.41 to 7,751.94. The narrow range is reported rather than hidden:
v0.8 must establish that its tasks discriminate meaningfully across these regimes before any model
comparison is publishable.

## Interpretation and next evidence gate

The report passes if its digest verifies and the simulator responds deterministically to declared
regime and parameter changes without violating conservation or policy invariants. It does not pass
the future task-discrimination or external-validity gates. v0.8 must test shortcut baselines,
ceilings and floors, item difficulty, regime separation, and model-free policy ordering. External
retail validity would require separately governed data, a declared target population, and empirical
calibration beyond this synthetic release.
